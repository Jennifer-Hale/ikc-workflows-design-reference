H2 2025 Request status monitoring — design validation

Usability study validating the design for workflow request status monitoring (Epic 6082). Conducted December 2025 by Jennifer Hale.

Overview Positive findings Areas for improvement Next steps Deliverables

Overview

In early December 2025, four interviews were held with internal IBM participants to hear feedback on their workflow processes and to gather reactions to new designs for request status monitoring.

Positive findings

1 — Workflow status is easy to discover

Users easily found the status of their workflow through multiple entry points: the task list details, task title subheading, request progress indicator, and activity history.

2 — Expanding each request step tested well

Seeing details within an expanded step matched how users expected to interact. It wasn't always immediately discoverable unprompted, which is acceptable since the design will expand by default.

  • "I'd like to click on the Review down arrow to see what information you can give me." — P1
  • "Follows suit with other parts in the product." — P3
  • "To me this is concise." — P4

3 — Tooltips and status icons tested positively

Users responded as expected to tooltip hover interactions and status icons, and understood what to look for within each area for more specific information.

  • "The information that pops up, I think that's important." — P2
  • "It is definitely helpful." — P4

4 — Claimed status is clearly understood

Users recognised that a Claimed status means the assignee has acknowledged the task and plans to work on it — they will be accountable for its completion.

  • "It means he is now going to be personally responsible for approving this business term." — P1
  • "He's claimed it — that's the same thing as accepting assignment." — P3

5 — Activity history is valuable

Participants quickly discovered activity history as a place to scroll through all past actions. They expected to see past events and comments here.

  • "The latest activity itself will be on the top and then of course listing the other ones below." — P2
  • "I can always click on older activities and probably see the whole workflow." — P4

6 — All users wanted to click "See details"

Every participant at some point said they'd click See details to learn more about the request process and status. This raises a question: is this potentially misleading, or a useful affordance to lean into?

  • "I also see a See details on the screen so I would also want to see the details." — P1
  • "If I want to see details, I just click on the little See details area." — P2

Areas for improvement

1 — Tasks with multiple assignees confuse users

Participants were generally confused by steps with multiple assignees — they didn't understand the symbols, names, user groups, and how these fit together. Specific feedback included:

  • Difficulty understanding which assignees still need to take action vs. who has completed theirs
  • Desire to show only assignees who still need to act
  • Concern around very long lists (10+ names) in large user groups
  • Suggestion to make names hyperlinks to launch email or a message
  • "Why is there still an In progress symbol next to the security team and GCDO?" — P1 & P2
  • "Who else is supposed to approve it? This doesn't tell me." — P4

2 — Task step names are confused with completed actions

The step name "Approve" was misleading — users assumed it was the only action available, and found it redundant when the tooltip showed the same word.

  • "Seeing the action is the same as the word itself." — P1
  • "By hovering over the icon it's going to give me the same word that I see right next to it." — P4

Proposed fix: Rename task steps to differentiate from action names — e.g. "Review step", "Approve step" — to better indicate these are stages, not completed actions.

3 — No easy way to escalate a stuck request

Multiple participants wished for a simple mechanism to unstick a request — a button or comment field to prompt the current assignee.

  • "I should have an action for me in the field — a button that says Request clarification, Escalate, whatever the word is." — P4
  • "I do wish those were links so I could click that person and go into their task and send them a message." — P3

Note from 3-in-a-Box (12/11): Consider a Reference ID to bring a request into context outside the Task Inbox.

4 — Negative action status icons should use a warning color

Users expected a rejected or failed step to show a red or warning-colored icon — not blue, which felt too neutral or positive.

  • "If it's rejected, it would be like a red cross and say not approved." — P1
  • "I want a different color. I don't want blue because blue is happy." — P3

Next steps

ActionEffort
Revise task step names — Rename "Approve" and similar steps so they don't read as completed actions. Use "Review step", "Approve step", or similar. Easy
Revise negative action status icon to warning color — Use red or warning token when a step was completed with a negative action (e.g. rejected). Needs deeper examination. Easy
Consider a simple escalation mechanism — Allow request authors to click a button to escalate: send assignee an email, flag as high priority, or leave a comment with an audit trail. Medium
Keep focus on what still needs to be done — Surface blockers and pending actions prominently in the complex request workflow view. Medium
Multiple enhancements for complex workflows — Show "3 of 5 completed" at a high level; consider user group tooltip for partial completion; address display of large user groups without lengthening the page. Hard
